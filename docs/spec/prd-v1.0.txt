1. Executive Summary

System Overview
This specification defines a two-layer, open-source M-Pesa (Safaricom Daraja)
state-reconciliation engine and Cashu payment processor written in Rust, designed for
native embedding or language-agnostic consumption over gRPC and REST:
   1.​ daraja-core (The Rust State Machine & Language-Agnostic Sidecar Daemon): A
       standalone, ACID-compliant Rust library crate and local daemon (daraja-daemon)
       wrapping Safaricom's Daraja APIs: STK Push (Lipa na M-Pesa Online), C2B
       (RegisterURL), STK Push Query, B2C v3 Disbursements (to phone numbers), B2B
       v1 Disbursements (BusinessBuyGoods for Merchant Tills and BusinessPayBill
       for PayBills), Account Balance Query, and Transaction Status Query. While
       implemented in Rust for compile-time state safety and single-binary deployment,
       daraja-daemon exposes a language-agnostic gRPC and HTTP/REST interface
       alongside an AGENTS.md integration skill so external platforms written in
       TypeScript/Node.js, Python, Go, C#, or PHP can consume idempotent M-Pesa
       collections and disbursements without writing telecom state machines.
   2.​ cdk-daraja-processor (The Cashu Mint gRPC Plugin): A Rust gRPC server
       implementing the cdk_payment_processor.CdkPaymentProcessor protobuf
       service for cdk-mintd. It enables Cashu mints to accept M-Pesa deposits to mint
       Chaumian ecash (KES or sat units) and disburse M-Pesa via B2C (to phone
       numbers) or B2B (directly to Merchant Tills and PayBills) when users melt ecash
       proofs.


Core Architectural Value & Structural Moat
   1.​ Idempotent Ambiguity Resolution (The Proof-to-Payout Lock): Connecting an
       irreversible, cryptographically blind bearer asset (Cashu) to an asynchronous
       telecom API (Daraja) creates severe double-spend and insolvency risks. If Daraja
       returns HTTP 500/503, drops a connection, or fires a QueueTimeOutURL callback,
       a naive adapter that marks the payout as Failed will unlock the user's ecash
       proofs while Safaricom still executes the transfer, draining the mint anonymously.
       This system enforces an UNKNOWN_IN_FLIGHT saga state that keeps ecash proofs
       locked in PENDING state and resolves ambiguity through deterministic
       OriginatorConversationID duplicate-rejection probing (500.002.1001) and
       out-of-band status reconciliation.
   2.​ Off-Rail Bearer Transfers at Zero Marginal Cost: Standard M-Pesa transfers incur
       tariffs on every peer-to-peer hop. By converting an M-Pesa deposit into Chaumian
       blind-signed bearer tokens (cashuB...), value can transfer across unlimited
       peer-to-peer or API-to-API hops over QR codes, WhatsApp, SMS, or HTTP headers
       at 0% fee and with mathematical transaction blindness, touching Safaricom's rails
       only at initial deposit and final settlement.
   3.​ Consumer MSISDN Privacy & Anti-Spam Shield (B2B Exit): Standard C2B Till and
       PayBill payments expose the payer's personal phone number and identity to
       merchant marketing databases. When melting Cashu tokens to a Till
       (BusinessBuyGoods) or PayBill (BusinessPayBill) via cdk-daraja-processor,
       the payment executes over Daraja B2B from the Mint's Corporate Shortcode. The
       merchant receives guaranteed settlement and the user receives the official M-Pesa
       TransactionID receipt code, while the user's personal phone number remains
       completely masked from third-party merchants.
   4.​ Elimination of Aggregator Counterparty Risk & Fees: Licensed operators and
       merchants holding their own Safaricom C2B, B2C, and B2B shortcodes can deploy
       this self-hosted binary directly against Daraja at 0% software fee, eliminating
       third-party payment aggregator fees and custodial float exposure.
   5.​ Build Once in Rust, Connect from Any Stack in 60 Seconds: By compiling to a
       static ~15MB Rust binary, exposing clean gRPC/REST socket boundaries, and
       shipping a structured AGENTS.md specification for coding agents (Claude Code,
       Cursor, Codex), the engine serves both Rust-native ecosystems (cdk-mintd) and
       polyglot web backends (TypeScript/Next.js, Python/Django/FastAPI, Go,
       PHP/Laravel, C#).

   ●​ Non-Custodial Software Distribution: This project is released solely as
      open-source, self-hosted software infrastructure under MIT/Apache-2.0 licenses.
      The software authors do not custody funds, hold Daraja keys, operate a shared
      gateway, or intermediate transactions. Each deploying entity supplies its own
      Safaricom credentials, corporate shortcodes, and settlement bank accounts.
   ●​ KES-Denominated Mints (National Payment System Act & 2026 Bill): Under the
      National Payment System (NPS) Act (2011) and the Draft National Payment
      System Policy & National Payment System Bill, 2026 (public consultation closed
      October 9, 2026), electronic money (e-money) is defined as monetary value
      represented by a claim on the issuer, stored electronically, issued on receipt of
      funds, and accepted as a means of payment by entities other than the issuer. A
       public mint issuing KES-denominated tokens redeemable at third-party merchant
       Tills or PayBills constitutes e-money issuance and Payment Service Provider (PSP)
       activity requiring Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) authorization (or admission into the
       CBK Regulatory Sandbox introduced under the 2026 NPS Bill), unless restricted to
       a legally exempt closed-loop environment.
   ●​ Crypto/SAT-Denominated Mints & Bridges (VASP Act 2025 & Regulations 2026):

      gazetted July 22, 2026; statutory transition deadline November 4, 2026), entities
      operating virtual asset payment processing, conversion, or custody in or from
      Kenya are subject to dual CBK/CMA supervision and Part IX / Fifth Schedule
      capital requirements:
          ○​ Virtual Asset Payment Processor (CBK): Minimum paid-up capital of KES
              10,000,000 (plus liquid capital of KES 4,000,000 or 8% of total liabilities,
              whichever is higher).
          ○​ Virtual Asset Broker / Tokenisation Platform (CMA): Minimum paid-up
              capital of KES 10,000,000.



           ○​ Virtual Asset Manager / ICO Provider (CMA): Minimum paid-up capital of

           ○​ Virtual Asset Exchange (CMA): Minimum paid-up capital of KES

           ○​ Virtual Asset Wallet Provider (CBK): Minimum paid-up capital of KES
              150,000,000.
           ○​ Stablecoin Issuer (CBK): Minimum paid-up capital of KES 300,000,000.

5.2 Mint Flow (Deposit via STK Push & C2B Fallback) State Machine

1. Quote Creation & Dual-Rail Checkout UX (CreatePayment)
   1.​ Client requests a mint quote for amount A (in KES or sat via pluggable FX quote
       oracle with a strict expiration TTL).
   2.​ The processor generates an 8-character alphanumeric account_ref (e.g.,
       CDK9F2A1), inserts a record into inbound_quotes with state ISSUED, expected
       amount = A, and shortcode.
   3.​ If an MSISDN is provided in request options, the processor dispatches
       mpesa/stkpush/v1/processrequest, stores the returned CheckoutRequestID,
       transitions state to STK_SENT, and returns a structured dual-rail response
       containing:
          ○​ 1. Active CheckoutRequestID status for the STK Push spinner.
          ○​ 2. Immediate One-Tap Copy C2B Fallback Instructions (PayBill
              Shortcode + account_ref + exact Amount), allowing the user to pay
              manually from the M-Pesa app or SIM Toolkit (or from a secondary phone
              number) if GSM signaling drops the STK prompt.
          ○​ 3. A 35-second STK Retry Cooldown timestamp synced to the first
              stkpushquery poll so the client UI only enables "Resend STK Prompt" once
              Safaricom's subscriber session lock has cleared.

2. Callback Ingestion & 4-Gate Zero-Trust Verification
When /callbacks/stk/{secret} or /callbacks/c2b/confirmation/{secret}
receives a payload with ResultCode: 0 and MpesaReceiptNumber:
   4.​ Gate 1 (Cryptographic URL Secret & Quote Binding): Verify the URL {secret}
       token in constant time. Match CheckoutRequestID (for STK) or BillRefNumber
       == account_ref (for C2B) against inbound_quotes, and verify
       BusinessShortCode matches the configured collection shortcode.
   5.​ Gate 2 (ACID Receipt Deduplication): Insert MpesaReceiptNumber into
       mpesa_receipts_ledger (UNIQUE constraint). If a conflict occurs, drop the
       callback immediately to prevent replay attacks.
   6.​ Gate 3 (Exact Amount Verification): Verify callback Amount ==
       expected_amount. If mismatched or expired, route immediately to the Exception
       State Machine (Section 5.2.4).
   7.​ Gate 4 (Mandatory Out-of-Band Verification for All Amounts): Because Daraja
       callbacks are unsigned, the engine verifies every transaction (regardless of KES
       amount) before minting:
          ○​ STK Push Path: Query mpesa/stkpushquery/v1/query (using exponential
              backoff if throttled by Daraja spike arrest 500.001.1001).
          ○​ C2B PayBill Path: Because stkpushquery has no visibility into manual C2B
              PayBill deposits, verify via mpesa/transactionstatus/v1/query using
              TransactionID = MpesaReceiptNumber (or via validated C2B
              Confirmation correlation when synchronous ValidationURL pre-check is
              enabled).

3. Active Polling Loop & Settlement Emission
   8.​ If a quote remains in STK_SENT for 35 seconds without a callback, reconciler.rs
       polls mpesa/stkpushquery/v1/query at 35s, 65s, and 110s.
   9.​ Upon verification, state transitions to PAID, and WaitIncomingPayment streams
       the settled quote identifier to cdk-mintd so the user can mint blinded tokens.

4. Missing Money Paths, Pre-Validation & Reversal Defense (unreconciled_deposits)
Manual C2B PayBill payments frequently deviate from quote parameters. To eliminate the
common M-Pesa failure mode where users send funds to a mistyped PayBill account
number and cannot recover them, daraja-core enforces both synchronous
pre-validation and post-settlement exception queues:
   1.​ Synchronous C2B Typo Rejection (ValidationURL): When a user initiates a
       manual C2B PayBill transfer, Safaricom calls
       /callbacks/c2b/validation/{secret} before deducting funds. daraja-core
       checks that BillRefNumber matches an active ISSUED or STK_SENT quote
       (account_ref) and that TransAmount == expected_amount. If no matching
       quote exists (e.g., user typo), daraja-core returns a non-zero rejection code
       (ResultCode: "C2B00012" Invalid Account Number or "C2B00013" Invalid
       Amount), causing M-Pesa to abort the transaction immediately before money
       leaves the user's phone.
   2.​ Underpayment (Amount < expected_amount): If C2B validation is bypassed or
       times out and an underpayment lands, the quote transitions to UNDERPAID_HELD.
       No ecash is minted. The user/operator can either: (a) apply a second C2B top-up
       referencing the same account_ref to reach expected_amount, or (b) trigger an
       automated B2C refund of Amount - b2c_tariff_fee.
   3.​ Overpayment (Amount > expected_amount): Quote transitions to
       OVERPAID_HELD (or optionally settles expected_amount and records Amount -
       expected_amount as a refundable credit in unreconciled_deposits).
   4.​ Late Deposit (Post-TTL) or Orphan C2B (BillRefNumber Not Found): Recorded
       as ORPHAN_UNCLAIMED in unreconciled_deposits. For KES-unit quotes (zero FX
       risk), operators can configure an automatic late-settlement grace window; for
       sat-unit quotes where the FX quote expired, funds remain held for manual refund
       or re-quoting at current spot rate.
   5.​ Post-Mint M-Pesa Reversal Defense: Because minted Chaumian ecash is
        bearer-portable and anonymous, the mint cannot claw back tokens if a depositor
        initiates a fraudulent M-Pesa reversal after spending ecash. To mitigate this:
   10.​Operators must configure their Safaricom C2B shortcode for Maker-Checker /
        Organization-Approved Reversals Only (blocking unilateral customer reversals).
   11.​ Configurable velocity limits and settlement hold windows apply to high-value
        deposits from unverified MSISDNs.




5.3 Melt Flow (Payout via B2C v3 & B2B v1) & Saga Recovery

1. Destination Parsing, Tariff Reservation & Anti-Spam Privacy (GetPaymentQuote)
The processor parses the melt target and computes the exact Safaricom corporate tariff
from tariffs.rs:
   12.​Phone Number (2547XXXXXXXX / 01XXXXXXXX): Routes to
       mpesa/b2c/v3/paymentrequest (CommandID: BusinessPayment, PartyB:
       <phone>).
   13.​Merchant Till (TILL:<till_number>): Routes to
       mpesa/b2b/v1/paymentrequest (CommandID: BusinessBuyGoods,
       SenderIdentifierType: 4, RecieverIdentifierType: 2).
   14.​Merchant PayBill (PAYBILL:<shortcode>:<account_ref>): Routes to
       mpesa/b2b/v1/paymentrequest (CommandID: BusinessPayBill,
       SenderIdentifierType: 4, RecieverIdentifierType: 4,
       AccountReference: <account_ref>).
   15.​Remitter Identity Masking & Cashier Proof: B2B payouts originate from the Mint's
       Corporate Shortcode, completely hiding the user's personal phone number from
       the receiving merchant or SACCO/bank PayBill and preventing SMS spam
       harvesting. Upon settlement, the processor returns the M-Pesa TransactionID
       (Receipt Number) in the payment proof/preimage field so the user can display the
       exact M-Pesa receipt code to the merchant or cashier.
2. Pre-Flight Lock & Dispatch (MakePayment)
     16.​cdk-mintd locks the user's ecash proofs in PENDING state and invokes
        MakePayment.
     17.​daraja-core generates a deterministic UUID (OriginatorConversationID),
        commits a row to outbound_payouts in state DISPATCHING prior to network I/O,
        and sends the B2C v3 or B2B v1 request.
     18.​Note on OriginatorConversationID tracking: daraja-core records both the
        client-supplied OriginatorConversationID and the synchronous response
        OriginatorConversationID / ConversationID returned by Daraja's proxy to
        ensure callback correlation succeeds across both API versions.

■​      3. The Ambiguous Timeout Trap & Two-Stage Resolution
     19.​Strict Invariant: The processor must NEVER return PaymentStatus::Failed to
        cdk-mintd on an HTTP 500, HTTP 503, network timeout, or Daraja
        QueueTimeOutURL callback. Returning Failed unlocks the user's ecash proofs
        while Safaricom may still execute the payout.
     20.​Instead, the processor transitions the record to UNKNOWN_IN_FLIGHT and returns
        PaymentStatus::Pending to cdk-mintd.
     21.​Two-Stage Ambiguity Resolver:
           ○​ Stage 1 — Idempotent Duplicate-Rejection Probe (500.002.1001): When an
               initial HTTP request times out without a synchronous acknowledgment,
               reconciler.rs re-submits the request with the exact same
               OriginatorConversationID. Under Daraja's idempotency rules, if the first
               request reached Safaricom, Daraja rejects the retry with error code
               500.002.1001 (Duplicate OriginatorConversationID / Duplicate
               Detected), proving the first payout is in flight; if the first request dropped
               before reaching Daraja, the retry executes safely once. (Sandbox Gate:
               Verified on B2C v3; must be integration-verified on B2B v1 during Phase 0
               prior to enabling B2B auto-retry).
            ○​ Stage 2 — TransactionStatus Polling & Bounded Pending TTL: For
               acknowledged in-flight transactions whose result callback dropped,
               reconciler.rs polls mpesa/transactionstatus/v1/query. Because
               TransactionStatus is asynchronous and cannot indefinitely block user
               funds if a transaction never registered, daraja-core enforces a
               configurable MAX_PENDING_TTL (e.g., 2 hours automated polling, 12 hours
               hard cutoff). If unresolved after MAX_PENDING_TTL, the payout transitions to
               MANUAL_RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED and triggers an operator alert to
               verify against the M-Pesa Organization Portal before marking the payout
               PAID (burning proofs) or FAILED (unlocking proofs).
6. Operational Liquidity: Solving the "Double-Float" Problem
Safaricom isolates C2B Customer Collection accounts (Utility/MMF) from B2C/B2B
Disbursement Working accounts:


   22.​Automated Balance Telemetry: daraja-core periodically queries
      mpesa/accountbalance/v1/query and exports Prometheus metrics
      (daraja_b2c_utility_balance_kes, daraja_c2b_collection_balance_kes).
   23.​Pre-Quote Melt Circuit Breaker: If daraja_b2c_utility_balance_kes falls
      below MIN_FLOAT_THRESHOLD + quote_amount, GetPaymentQuote rejects new
      melt requests upfront (Disbursement liquidity rebalancing in progress)
      rather than locking user proofs and failing mid-flight.
   24.​Optional Internal Float Sweep: For enterprise M-Pesa organizations where C2B
       and B2C shortcodes are linked under the same Head Office portal hierarchy,
      daraja-core supports an optional automated trigger via
      mpesa/b2b/v1/paymentrequest (CommandID:
      BusinessTransferFromMMFToUtility). For standard unlinked shortcodes,
      low-float alerts notify the operator to initiate a bank/PesaLink transfer.
